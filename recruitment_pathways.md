# Recruitment Pathways

## Overview

This section examines how individuals are introduced, evaluated, and integrated into decentralized cyber threat actor ecosystems.

Recruitment is typically informal, gradual, and behavior-driven rather than structured or explicit.

---

## Entry Points

Common entry points include:

* Gaming communities
* Social media platforms
* Public Discord servers

These environments provide:

* Low barriers to entry
* High volume of potential participants
* Opportunities for passive observation

---

## Initial Engagement

Early-stage interaction is characterized by:

* Casual communication
* Observation of group dynamics
* Limited participation

At this stage, individuals are not formally recruited but are exposed to the environment.

---

## Trust Building

Progression depends on:

* Consistent interaction
* Alignment with group norms
* Demonstration of knowledge or interest

Trust is developed informally through repeated engagement.

---

## Gradual Escalation

As trust increases, individuals may:

* Gain access to additional channels or groups
* Be included in more focused discussions
* Participate in task-oriented communication

This creates a layered access structure without formal onboarding.

---

## Informal Vetting

Participants are often evaluated based on:

* Communication behavior
* Reliability and responsiveness
* Perceived capability

This process is implicit rather than documented.

---

## Role Transition

Over time, individuals may transition from:

* Observer → participant → contributor

This progression reinforces both identity and commitment to the ecosystem.

---

## Behavioral Reinforcement

Recruitment is sustained through:

* Social validation
* Recognition from higher-status individuals
* Access to information or capabilities

These factors encourage continued participation and escalation.

---

## Key Insight

Recruitment in these ecosystems is not a single event.

It is a process of:

* exposure
* engagement
* integration

driven by behavior and social dynamics.

---

## Summary

This recruitment model demonstrates:

* How decentralized groups grow without formal structure
* The role of trust and interaction in onboarding
* The importance of gradual escalation in sustaining participation
